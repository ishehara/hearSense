Component 03

📝 Sign Language Reader

An advanced computer vision system that watches a person signing in real time and converts Sinhala Sign Language gestures into text — using hands, face, and body together

← Back to Home

What Does This Component Do?

Real-time Sinhala Sign Language recognition through a standard camera

This component allows a computer or mobile device to watch a person performing Sinhala Sign Language through a camera and understand what sign they are making — in real time.

When a deaf or hearing-impaired person signs in front of the camera, the system watches their hand gestures, facial expressions, and body posture all at the same time, figures out which Sinhala word or phrase is being signed, and displays the meaning on the screen. It does not require any special hardware — just a regular camera.

Why Does It Exist?

Breaking the communication barrier between deaf and hearing communities

🚧 The Communication Barrier

Deaf and hearing-impaired people in Sri Lanka use Sinhala Sign Language to communicate. However, most people around them — including family members, teachers, doctors, and strangers — do not know sign language.

This creates a communication barrier in everyday life where even simple interactions become a challenge.

🌉 Breaking the Barrier

This component acts as an automatic interpreter that can read sign language and turn it into text, so that anyone with a phone or computer can understand what a deaf person is trying to say — without needing to learn sign language themselves.

Why Hands Alone Are Not Enough

True sign language comprehension requires reading the whole body

Most existing sign language recognition systems only look at hand gestures. However, in real sign language, meaning is carried not just by the hands but also by the face and the body. A signer's facial expression can completely change the meaning of a sign — for example, the same hand movement can mean a question or a statement depending on the eyebrow and mouth position.

Similarly, body posture and shoulder movement add important context that hand gestures alone cannot convey. By reading all three signals together — hand gestures, facial expressions, and body posture — this component produces significantly more accurate results than systems that rely on hands only. This mirrors how a real, experienced interpreter reads sign language.

How Does It Work?

Six steps from camera input to Sinhala text output

1

Camera Watches

The camera watches the person signing live, capturing continuous video input.

2

Three Signals Tracked

Simultaneously tracks hand gestures, facial expressions, and body posture in parallel.

3

Motion Over Time

Looks at how all three signals change together over time — not a single image, but the full motion of the sign from start to finish.

4

Unified Understanding

Combines information from hands, face, and body into a single unified interpretation of the sign being performed.

5

Pattern Matching

Compares what it sees to hundreds of Sinhala signs it has already learned and picks the closest match.

6

Text Displayed

The recognised sign is shown on screen as Sinhala text, along with the system's confidence level. All in real time with no delay.

Where Is It Used?

Designed for real everyday situations, not just laboratory environments

This component runs as part of a mobile application. A person holds up their phone, the camera sees the signing, and the app shows the recognised word or phrase on screen. It is designed to work in real everyday situations — in homes, schools, hospitals, shops, and public spaces.

No special hardware is required — just a standard smartphone camera. The system processes everything on-device in real time, meaning no internet connection is needed and results appear within moments of the sign being performed.

Overall Purpose

The Sign Language Reader breaks down the most fundamental communication barrier between deaf and hearing communities. By reading hands, face, and body together — mirroring how a real interpreter works — it achieves far superior accuracy and enables natural, real-time communication without any intermediary.
